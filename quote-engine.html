The formula

Six things make up a price

They are added together, then VAT is applied to the lot. Nothing is rounded until the end, so 23 miles is £34.50, not £35.

SUBTOTAL = Call-out + Labour + Mileage + Inventory + Access + Add-ons
VAT = SUBTOTAL × 20%
TOTAL = SUBTOTAL + VAT

where
Call-out = £40 — waived when the job is 4 hours or less
Labour = hours × crew hourly rate
Mileage = miles × £1.50
Inventory = each item's price × how many
Access = stairs, no lift, loft, cellar, long carry, parking
Add-ons = packing, dismantling, storage handling

The call-out rule means a short job stands on its own labour. A job over four hours picks up the £40 on top.

Only stairs and no-lift are asked for on the website

Loft, cellar, long carry, difficult parking and extra trips are all priced in the engine, but the customer is never asked about them. The office adds them when confirming. A quote that needs them will come out low until it is confirmed.

Hours and crew

Where the hours come from

The spreadsheet has you type the hours and crew in. A customer can't be asked that, so the site works them out — and whatever the office sends in overrides it.

hours = travel time + handling time
travel time = miles ÷ 60 mph (rounded to the nearest half hour)
handling time = 0 when the customer has itemised the move
                = from the property size when they haven't

Every inventory item is already priced for the work of carrying it. Charging property-size handling hours on top of that would bill the same sofa twice, so handling only applies when nothing has been itemised.

Watch these

Where the numbers still look wrong

The arithmetic is right in all ten cases above. These two are rate-card decisions, not faults in the calculation.

Small local jobs come out very low

Three items moved six miles prices at £67.20, and a single washing machine at £35.40. With no hours on the job there is no labour and no call-out, so only the item prices and mileage remain — a crew and a van for a morning for £56 before VAT. A minimum job charge would set the floor.

Packing is a flat £180 whatever the size

On the six-mile, three-item move it is 64% of the bill and takes the quote to £283.20. The £180 rate is built for packing a whole house, not three pieces of furniture. Scaling it with the inventory, or using the £75 fragile-only rate below a threshold, would bring that job into the £200–250 range.

Reference

Quote references and email

  • Customer quotes get a reference like VH-261107-4P6W — VH, the date, then four characters.
  • Driver applications use VH-APP-261107-KEGW.
  • The office copy goes to Vmtofficials@gmail.com with the full breakdown, the itemised inventory and the customer's own description. Replying to it reaches the customer.
  • The customer's copy shows the total only — never the line-by-line breakdown.
  • Quotes send from quotes@vanhero.online, driver applications from partners@vanhero.online.
  • A failed send is retried once and logged with the lead's name and number, so a lost email can still be chased.
